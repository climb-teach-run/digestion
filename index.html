The processes of digestion

ProcessWhat it meansWhere it mainly happens
IngestionTaking food into the bodyMouth
Mechanical digestionPhysically breaking food into smaller pieces without changing the molecules (chewing, churning, emulsifying fat with bile)Mouth, stomach, small intestine
PropulsionMoving food along: swallowing, then peristalsis (waves of muscle contraction)Pharynx, esophagus, stomach, intestines
Chemical digestionEnzymes break large molecules into small ones (starch → glucose, protein → amino acids, fat → fatty acids + glycerol)Mouth, stomach, small intestine
AbsorptionSmall nutrient molecules pass through the intestine wall into the blood or lymphSmall intestine (nutrients); large intestine (water, minerals)
AssimilationBody cells take in and use the absorbed nutrients for energy, growth, and repairCells all over the body
Egestion (elimination)Removing undigested material as fecesRectum and anus

Aligned with the DepEd Grade 8 Science competency: "Explain ingestion, absorption, assimilation, and excretion" (S8LT-IVa-13). See the References tab for sources.

Word check: Some textbooks call the removal of feces "excretion." Strictly, excretion means removing wastes made by cells (like urea or carbon dioxide). Feces are mostly food that was never absorbed, so scientists call its removal egestion or elimination.

Mechanical vs. chemical digestion

Mechanical digestionChemical digestion
What changesSize of food pieces onlyThe molecules themselves
HowTeeth, tongue, stomach muscles, bileEnzymes (helped by stomach acid)
Why it mattersMore surface area for enzymes to work onMakes molecules small enough to be absorbed
ExampleMolars grinding riceSalivary amylase turning starch into maltose

Enzyme summary

EnzymeMade byWorks inBreaks down → intoBest pH (approx.)
Salivary amylaseSalivary glandsMouthStarch → maltose (and shorter starch chains)≈ 6.8–7
PepsinStomach lining (released as inactive pepsinogen, activated by stomach acid)StomachProteins → polypeptides (shorter chains)≈ 1.5–2
Pancreatic amylasePancreasSmall intestineStarch → maltose≈ 7–8
TrypsinPancreas (released as inactive trypsinogen)Small intestineProteins and polypeptides → peptides≈ 8
Pancreatic lipasePancreasSmall intestineFats → fatty acids + glycerol*≈ 8 (works far better with bile)
PeptidasesSmall intestine lining (and pancreas)Small intestinePeptides → amino acids≈ 7–8
MaltaseSmall intestine liningSmall intestineMaltose → glucose + glucose≈ 6–7†
SucraseSmall intestine liningSmall intestineSucrose → glucose + fructose≈ 5–7†
LactaseSmall intestine liningSmall intestineLactose → glucose + galactose≈ 5.5–6†
Bile is not an enzyme. It is made by the liver and stored in the gallbladder. It does not change fat molecules; it breaks big fat droplets into tiny ones (emulsification), which gives lipase more surface to work on.
* Strictly, lipase often leaves one fatty acid still attached to glycerol (a monoglyceride). Grade 8 texts simplify this to "fatty acids and glycerol."
Other fat enzymes: small amounts of lipase from the tongue (lingual lipase) and stomach (gastric lipase) start fat digestion early, but pancreatic lipase does most of the work.
pH values are approximate and differ between sources; † values for brush-border enzymes vary the most.
